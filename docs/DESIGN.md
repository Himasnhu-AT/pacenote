# Pacenote — design

An original two-person rally game on `engine`. A **driver** and a **guide** race one car
through point-to-point stages. The driver sees only the road; the guide sees the stage map
and the car on it but can't touch the controls. They talk over in-game voice. Teams compete on
per-stage leaderboards.

## Pillars

1. **Trust and talk.** The fun is the conversation: the guide reads the road ahead ("left four
   into right two, don't cut, crest"), the driver commits. Neither can win alone.
2. **Driving that feels earned.** Weight transfer, slides on loose gravel, ruts, crests that go
   light. Mistakes cost time, not restarts.
3. **Stages worth learning.** 20 fixed stages (fixed seeds), each with its own character.
   Times are comparable because everyone drives the same 20.

## Roles

| | Driver | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Sees | Car view (bonnet or chase cam), dashboard, stage clock | Live stage map (top-down, upcoming 500 m highlighted), car position/speed/heading, distance to next corner, a corner list with auto-graded severity, split times |
| Controls | Steer, throttle, brake, handbrake, gear (auto or manual), horn | Map zoom/pan, mark hazards on their own map, push-to-talk |
| Never sees | The map, the corner list | The car camera (only a small rear-view thumbnail would be cheating: none) |
| Voice | Open mic or push-to-talk | Push-to-talk (default) |

The guide's corner list is graded from the road geometry (1 = hairpin … 6 = flat-out kink, plus
crests, jumps, narrows, "don't cut"), the same scale real co-drivers use; reading it out in time
is the guide's skill.

## Stages

- **20 stages, fixed seeds** (`stages.ron`): length 2–6 km, surface mix (gravel, tarmac, mud,
  snow), biome (pine forest, birch hills, dry Mediterranean scrub, highland moor, snow forest),
  time of day and weather. Seeds never change once published, so leaderboards stay valid; a
  new stage gets a new id.
- **Generated by `engine::pcg` and the world module** from the seed:
  1. Terrain heightfield (fBm + ridges) for the biome.
  2. Road centreline: a spline grown across the terrain by constrained random walk (max grade,
     curvature budget, crests where the terrain rises), relaxed so it hugs the land.
  3. Road mesh: banking from curvature, camber, width variation (narrows), ruts; surface
     material per segment; shoulders and ditches cut into the terrain.
  4. Dressing (PCG graph): trees and rocks by biome density, kept clear of the road; fences,
     posts, hay bales and spectator areas at corners; stage start/finish arches, split
     markers.
  5. Pace data: corners extracted from curvature → severity, length, direction, modifiers.
- Deterministic: same seed → same stage on every machine (tested by hashing the generated
  road and placement).

## Driving model

- Raycast suspension per wheel (spring, damper, anti-roll bar), a rigid body chassis (avian).
- Tyre model: slip ratio / slip angle with a simplified Pacejka curve per surface (grip,
  peak, falloff), combined slip via friction ellipse, rolling resistance and loose-surface drag.
- Drivetrain: engine torque curve, 5-speed gearbox (auto with clutch logic, or manual),
  differential (4WD with centre split, viscous limited-slip front, rear and centre so an
  unloaded wheel can't take the drive), handbrake locks the rear and cuts the rear coupling.
  Hill hold: stopped with no pedals, the brakes hold the car (sloped start lines).
- No damage: the car bounces off big trees and boulders, snaps saplings, marker posts and
  kilometre boards (they topple; it loses only their momentum) and pushes through shrubs (a
  little drag). A recover button (5 s penalty) resets the car to the road.
- Input through `engine::actions`: gamepad (analogue throttle/brake/steer) and keyboard with
  steering assist.

## Multiplayer and voice

- The **driver hosts** (engine `net`, listen server): physics is authoritative there, so the
  driver never feels lag. The guide connects as a client and receives the replicated car
  state, stage id, clock and splits.
- **Voice** over the same connection: microphone capture with `cpal` (already in the tree via
  rodio), 16 kHz mono, compressed with a small built-in codec (IMA ADPCM, 4:1, ~64 kbit/s),
  sent on the unreliable channel in 20 ms frames, jitter-buffered and played through a
  `Voice` bus in `engine::sound` (ducking the car audio slightly while the guide speaks).
- Lobby: host creates a team session, shares an address (LAN, or a forwarded port over the
  internet; the engine has no relay yet), guide joins, both pick roles, the host picks a
  stage, countdown, go.
- Solo practice: the driver can drive alone with an automatic co-driver that reads the corner
  list (no leaderboard entry).

## Leaderboards

- Per stage: team (driver + guide names), time, penalties, date, build. Stored locally; when
  two teams connect their boards merge (newest per team kept). A shared online board is a
  later milestone (needs a small server).
- Ghost car of your team's best run, and of a selected rival team's run (inputs + seed replay).

## Look and audio

- Realistic CC0 / CC-BY assets (see `assets_src/SOURCES.md`, credits screen), cooked to KTX2
  with the engine cook. Car modelled from a downloaded glTF where licence allows, otherwise
  built in code.
- Engine `look`: atmosphere sky + HDRI-like ambient per stage time of day, fog for depth, dust
  and mud VFX from the wheels (`engine::vfx`), decals for tyre tracks, mud building up on the
  car.
- Audio: synthesised engine sound (RPM-driven layers), gravel/tarmac tyre loops by slip, gear
  whine, impacts, wind; the guide's voice on its own bus.

## Milestones (each one playable)

1. **Drive** — car physics on a generated stage, chase/bonnet cam, timing, 20 seeds, solo.
2. **Guide** — second instance joins over the network as guide: map view, corner list, splits.
3. **Voice** — mic capture, codec, jitter buffer, push-to-talk, voice bus.
4. **Look** — downloaded assets, materials, biomes, weather, dust/mud, audio.
5. **Compete** — leaderboards, ghosts, menus, lobby, settings, credits.
6. **Polish** — performance pass (governor, PCG density tiers), tests, packaging.

## Non-goals (for now)

Online matchmaking or relays, more than one car per stage, career mode, licensed cars or
brands, VR.
