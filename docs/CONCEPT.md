# Pacenote: game concept

Where the game is going, in one doc. Written 6 Nov 2026. `GAME_BRIEF.md` says what exists
today; this doc says what Pacenote should become. For the world see `OPENWORLD.md`, for
driving `HANDLING.md`, for progression `festival/`.

## In one line

**An open-world racer about dirt and supercars: rally the valley's gravel by day, run its
closed mountain roads in supercars by night, and build one garage of cars that look and feel
like they've lived through all of it.**

Open world, a festival you can drive around, and always something to do nearby, like Forza
Horizon. Driving that rewards learning the road, as in rally games. A second, rougher scene
after dark that gives the races stakes.

## Where the idea comes from

A reference video (Nov 2026) about a new open-world racer from a studio of ex-Forza Horizon
developers. Its pitch is *"Need for Speed Underground meets Forza Horizon"*. Three ideas in it
are worth taking:

1. **Stakes, not just scenery.** Open-world racers tend to be "a beautiful world to drive
   around" with nothing at risk. That game gives you two sides, a pro circuit and an
   underground scene, and makes you choose between them.
2. **Cars that look lived in.** Dust builds up, the exhaust tips go black, the seat leather
   sags. You can leave things in the cabin (a coffee cup in the holder, a hoodie on the
   passenger seat) so the car feels like yours.
3. **Competition is good.** Forza Horizon has had no real rival. There is room for more than
   one take on the open-world festival.

What we **don't** take: its setting (French Riviera), its crime story, gadgets (nitrous,
grappling hooks), police chases, its characters or its look. Pacenote stays an original
game: fictional valley, fictional cars, no licensed brands (`GAME_BRIEF.md`, Out of scope).
Our version of "stakes" is the road itself and what you risk on it, not cops.

## The two sides of the valley

The tension between a pro circuit and an underground scene becomes **day and night**, and
**dirt and tarmac**.

| | **The Festival** (day) | **The Late Run** (night) |
|---|---|---|
| Who runs it | The sanctioned rally festival in Fernhollow | Locals who close the mountain roads after dark |
| Surface | Gravel, forest tracks, river crossings, the quarry | The tarmac hillclimb, the B-road, the pass |
| Cars | Rally cars, 4×4s, rally-prepped supercars | Supercars and hot road cars |
| Format | Stages against the clock, with a co-driver's pace notes | Head-to-head runs, touge-style duels, top-speed pulls |
| What's at risk | Time. A mistake costs seconds, never a restart | Your car. Damage carries over until you repair it; some runs are for pink slips (see Open questions) |
| Mood | Bright, dusty, crowds at the hairpins | Headlights, wet tarmac, a few parked cars at the summit |
| Reward | Notes, Driver grade, Festival standing | Notes, rare parts, Late Run reputation, cars you can't buy |

Both sides use the same roads at different times. The hillclimb you learn by day as a timed
stage is the road you race wheel-to-wheel at 2 a.m. That's the point: **the more you drive a
road, the better you are on it**, whichever side you're on.

The player doesn't have to pick one side forever. But some things open only with standing on
one side (a Festival invitational, a Late Run crew's garage), so there is a reason to lean one
way.

## The two car fantasies

### Dirt

The core of Pacenote and what it already does well (`HANDLING.md`): weight transfer, gravel
slides, crests that go light, a co-driver reading the road.

- Rally cars from small front-drive hatches up to four-wheel-drive class S monsters.
- 4×4s and trucks for the rough tracks, river crossings and the quarry.
- **Supercars on dirt**: the stand-out fantasy. Raise a mid-engine supercar, fit gravel
  tyres and a skid plate, and take it down a forest stage. Fast, scary, and something few
  games let you do properly.

### Supercars

The second pillar, new for Pacenote.

- Mid-engine and front-engine supercars, a few hypercars at the top of class S. All
  fictional, each with its own engine character (flat-six, V8, V12, turbo four).
- Mainly tarmac: the hillclimb, the mountain pass and the B-road at night.
- Handled with the same tyre model as the rally cars, so a 600 hp car on cold tyres at night
  must be treated with respect. Supercars aren't easier. They're faster, and they punish
  mistakes harder.
- Every supercar can be built for dirt too (above). One car, two lives.

## Cars that feel like yours

Taken from the reference and adapted:

- **Wear you can read.** Dust and mud build up by surface (fine dust on gravel, mud after the
  river, brake dust on the wheels after the pass). Exhaust tips darken with miles. Tyres show
  wear. Washing the car at the Service Park is a choice, not something done for you.
- **Damage that sticks.** Already in the game for bodywork. In the Late Run it carries from
  run to run until you pay to repair it.
- **A lived-in cabin.** A short list of cabin items: a cup in the holder, a jacket on the
  seat, a hanging tag on the mirror, stickers, a stage map on the dash. Small, cheap to build,
  and visible in the in-car view and the showroom.
- **History.** Each car keeps its own record: km driven, km on gravel, stage wins, its best
  Late Run, its worst crash. Shown in the garage. A car with 2,000 km of gravel on it should
  look like it.

## Core loop

1. **Drive the valley.** Day or night, find an event marker, a roadside challenge, or a
   Late Run meet.
2. **Race.**
   - Day: Festival stages and rally sprints, with pace notes, against the clock or rivals.
   - Night: Late Run duels and runs on tarmac.
3. **Earn.** Notes, Driver grade, standing on either side, and Rhythm bonuses for style.
4. **Build the garage.** Buy cars, fit upgrades that change the physics, build a car for dirt
   or for tarmac, repair Late Run damage.
5. **Go harder.** Higher classes, longer stages, Late Run crews with better drivers, and in
   the end the valley's two finales (below).

## Pace notes stay the hook

The brief's open question is "what is the hook?" This concept keeps pace notes at the centre
on **both** sides:

- **Day:** the co-driver calls every Festival stage. Recce drives let you write your own
  notes for a road.
- **Night:** no co-driver. You race on the notes you wrote, or from memory. Driving the
  stage by day makes you faster at night. That ties the two sides together.
- **Co-op:** a friend as navigator, in the open world, on either side.

## Progression arc

| Stage | Roughly | What the player does |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | First 30 min | A rally hatch, the first Festival stages, a first look at a Late Run meet from the roadside |
| Rookie | 1–3 h | First 4×4 or rally car, first Late Run invite, a used supercar from the Dealership |
| Regular | 3–8 h | A garage of 4–6 cars, one built for dirt, one for tarmac; standing on both sides |
| Name in the valley | 8 h+ | Class S, rally-prepped supercars, Late Run crew leaders, Festival invitationals |
| Finales | End of the main arc | **The Festival Marathon:** a long rally across every gravel road. **The Summit Run:** one night duel up the pass against the best Late Run driver |

## Tone

- Grittier than a sunny festival, but not a crime story. Think mud on the bodywork, a
  headlight that flickers after a crash, a crowd at the hairpin who cheer when you go wide.
- Characters are few and grounded: your co-driver, a Festival steward, a Late Run organiser,
  a couple of rivals with names who remember how you raced them.
- Radio chatter and the co-driver's voice carry most of the story (`VOICE.md`).

## What already exists to build on

| Idea | In the game today |
|---|---|
| Open world | 4×4 km valley, 21 roads (~38 km), Fernhollow, traffic, day/night cycle |
| Dirt driving | Gravel/tarmac/loose/ditch surfaces, custom 60 Hz car sim, damage |
| Pace notes | Co-driver calls built from the road's curvature |
| Events | 5 routes as Race or Time Trial, 13 roadside challenges |
| Garage | 8 cars, 7 upgrade parts, PI classes D–S, Dealership, showroom |
| Style | 11 skills chaining into Rhythm, Driver grade 1–30 |
| Night | Day/night with car lights and street lamps |

## What's new

1. **The Late Run:** night events on tarmac (duels, runs), its own standing and rewards.
2. **Supercars:** 4–6 fictional supercars and a first hypercar, with tarmac and dirt builds.
3. **Wear and cabin items:** dust/mud by surface, darkening exhausts, tyre wear, washing,
   cabin items, per-car history.
4. **Damage that carries over** in the Late Run, with repair costs.
5. **Two finales** and standing on both sides.
6. **A road worth racing at night:** a mountain pass above the hillclimb, the one new road
   this concept needs.

## Open questions

- **Pink-slip runs:** can you really lose a car in the Late Run? Exciting, but it can feel
  awful. Option: only for cars won in the Late Run, never ones you bought.
- **How dark is the Late Run?** "Locals closing roads at night" is unsanctioned but no crime
  plot. Do we want marshals and spotters, or nothing but headlights?
- **Supercar count vs. feel.** The brief says Pacenote competes on feel, not size. Six
  supercars that each feel different beat twenty that don't.
- **Should day and night share one standing** or keep two? Two gives the choice weight; one
  is simpler.
- **Cops:** left out on purpose. Revisit only if the Late Run needs more risk than damage
  and reputation.
